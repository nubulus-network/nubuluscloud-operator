Two things have to exist before a tunnel can come up, and neither is in this chart.

1. A credential. Create an application token in the panel and store it in the
   namespace where the Tunnel will live:

     kubectl -n <namespace> create secret generic nubulus-api \
       --from-literal=client_id=<client id> \
       --from-literal=client_secret=<client secret>

   It is read from the Tunnel's own namespace and nowhere else, so each
   namespace brings its own account.

2. DNS. A route only carries traffic once its hostname points at the tunnel.
   After creating a Tunnel, read the target off it:

     kubectl -n <namespace> get tunnel <name> -o jsonpath='{.status.cnameTarget}'

   and publish that as a CNAME wherever the hostname's zone lives.

A minimal pair of objects:

    apiVersion: tunnel.nubulusnetwork.es/v1alpha1
    kind: Tunnel
    metadata:
      name: production
    spec:
      credentials:
        name: nubulus-api
    ---
    apiVersion: tunnel.nubulusnetwork.es/v1alpha1
    kind: TunnelRoute
    metadata:
      name: web
    spec:
      tunnelRef: production
      hostname: app.example.com
      service:
        name: web
        port: 8080

Then watch them settle:

    kubectl -n <namespace> get tunnels,tunnelroutes

BEFORE YOU UNINSTALL: delete every Tunnel and TunnelRoute first, and wait for
them to go. They carry finalizers, and the operator is what runs them. Removing
the operator first leaves objects that cannot be deleted and tunnels that stay
alive on the platform with nothing in this cluster pointing at them.
